The Anti-AI Toolkit

You’re Not Bad for Using AI

(But You Should Stop Using It Anyway)

AI Is Often Wrong

AI doesn't understand the words its writing. Instead, it strings together text based on things its seen before, and it can get these things wrong.

For example: Someone posts their Amazing Digital Circusart on Instagram and then use AI for the caption. The caption says the work is of of Jax, but its actually of Zooble. The characters look nothing alike, but the AI doesn't notice because they associate Jax with the show more.

AI will make mistakes you likely wouldn't have made.

AI Can't Create Anything New

A few weeks ago, Strauss Zelnick (chief executive of Take-Two Interactive, a video game parent company responsible for the Grand Theft Auto and Borderlands franchinses) broke down this point flawlessly:

"Remember what AI is, despite the fact that there are people in Silicon Valley who don't want you to believe this, is big data sets, lots of compute(rs), and a large language model mushed together. That's what they are. So, data sets by their very nature are backward looking. Creativity by its very nature is where we're looking. Creativity is informed by data."

You are more creative without AI since it cannot create, just modify.

AI Wants You to Become Reliant On It

Its cliché to compare the new scary thing to drugs, so I won't catastrophize the situation. However, many generative AI tools are being designed to make you addicted. An internal Microsoft document about their newest AI assistant, Scout, recently leaked to 404 Media, including their “three phases from addictive app to agentic platform” plan.

Additionally, researchers are beginning to see neurological effects caused by generative AI. The famous MIT Gen-AI study on this topic concluded that using AI could negatively impact students' “cognitive development, critical thinking, and intellectual independenc," which are key factors in your mental development.

Basically, your brain is a muscle: if you don't use it properly, it can atrophy. But like your other muscles, consistent exercise helps! AI is a convenient option that can have damaging consequences if used incorrectly.

Your work is stronger without AI, even if it feels like it isn't.

AI's Impact

Look, I know this whole page probably seems judgemental and preachy, but there are actual real world costs that we need to discuss.

AI is rapidly expanding and requires an immense amount of water and energy to function, to the point that it vastly outpaces normal human use. Energy prices are now skyrocketing and there's a real concern about water scarcity in the near future.

These are long term issues that are already effecting us. Govenor Mikie Sherrill issued a State-wide Drought Warning last month, which is only getting worse. And this is only the tip of the iceburg when it comes to all of the things AI is affecting.

Change doesn't happen alone nor in a vacuum. Its something we choose to build together over both time and combined effort.
